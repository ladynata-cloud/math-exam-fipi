## Mathematics and source caveats

All conditions, answers and every solution step were read and checked. Numeric conclusions are correct, including 386=2cm, 387=19.5cm, 388=10cm, 391=18.1cm, 392=1.9cm and 406=72°. In 385б, equal distances establish possibility, not automatic symmetry of every such pair. In 389 the equality or inequality of distance sums determines collinearity before the final geometry is disclosed.

The six pairs in figure 181 are faithfully distinguished: only the two triangles and MN/M₁N₁ are reflected pairs. The different line inclinations, unequal PQ/RS lengths, different ray origins/directions and unequal circle radii remain distractors, rather than being replaced with symmetric models.

The proofs use established elementary triangle equality, perpendicular and tangent properties, and reflection's preservation of distance. No Pythagorean theorem, similarity, trigonometric theorem or later general inscribed-angle theorem is used in learner solutions. Every didactic step has a specific prompt, reason and available theory reference; refs are within 1–45 and were semantically checked. Reusing a proved earlier numbered result is explicit and does not create a cycle.

- 395: both constant perimeter 2AB and angle 90°−½∠BAC are proved. Intersections with the *segments* select the arc facing A; its endpoints are explicitly excluded from the nondegenerate construction.
- 396: intersection of the two common tangent lines is justified; tangent lengths give the midpoint of AB and the desired circle. The distinct endpoint requirement is stated.
- 399: O≠M and O=M are separated, avoiding a degenerate perpendicular-bisector argument.
- 400: the circumcenter may be outside the triangle, and the alternate model preserves this case.
- 401: the unique center construction has both a verification and an existence/uniqueness argument.
- 402: an interior-strip point gives two centers; an exterior point gives none; boundary points are excluded by the source condition.
- 408: the printed statement lacks the necessary exception at X=O. The original wording is retained, with an explicit counterexample using two diameters and an arbitrary unsuitable diameter-axis. The noncentral proof excludes diameters before using nondegenerate right triangles. A separate central-case view shows the two valid bisector axes. The source caveat is **not** concealed or represented as a proof of the unqualified false statement.
- 410б: both allowable kinds of symmetry axes for a segment are retained: exchanging endpoints across p, and fixing every point of a segment contained in p. Existence, coincident lines, parallel lines and zero-length exclusions are addressed.
- 411б: candidates on the axis are excluded; coincident unordered segments are counted only once. Two symmetric intersections can yield the same segment in reverse order, and this has its own step and model case.
- 412: two axes exchange nonperpendicular lines; for the union of two perpendicular lines the original two lines are also axes, giving four. The narrower interpretation requiring exchange of the two lines is explicitly distinguished.
